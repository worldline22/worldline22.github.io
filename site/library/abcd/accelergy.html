01

First, picture the problem.

Counting hardware blocks alone misses how often they operate and whether those actions are reads, writes, gated cycles, or data-dependent computations.

↳An electricity bill depends on each appliance’s power use and how often it runs.
02

Make it concrete.

Move fewer values, spend less energy

Interactive · toy example

Our toy performs four multiplies at 1 energy unit each. A memory read costs 10. Compare four reads with one reused read; all other costs are deliberately omitted.

Four reads: 4 × 10 + 4 × 1 = 44 energy units. One reused read: 1 × 10 + 4 × 1 = 14 units. This simplified accounting omits local access, leakage, clocking, and interconnect costs.

Invented teaching example. Values and costs here are not measurements from the paper.

03

What did the paper add?

01

Describe components

Build architecture descriptions from primitive and compound components.

02

Price actions

Use estimation plug-ins and energy reference tables for distinct actions.

03

Accumulate activity

Combine action counts with costs to estimate total energy.

The contribution: An extensible architecture-level energy-estimation framework with hierarchical components and action-aware modeling.

04

Bring the idea to ABCD.

A possible research direction
Component hierarchy→Action counts × action costs→Energy estimate

Use action-based accounting when allocating or evaluating ABCD’s system-level energy budgets.

Small experiment: Compare two mappings with identical arithmetic but different memory traffic. Include communication and idle activity in the final estimate.

Keep in mind: Early estimates depend on models and activity counts. Summing module area or power cannot automatically capture every integration effect.

This connection is an interpretation for the project, not an implementation or performance claim by the paper.

05

Read the result in context.

Total-energy validation case
Within 5%

Reported against post-layout simulation for the evaluated Eyeriss-like DNN accelerator.

Model granularity
Per action

The evaluation also examines energy breakdowns and behavior such as zero gating.

Scope matters. The validation result is tied to the studied design and workloads. It does not establish a 5% error bound for arbitrary architectures or estimation plug-ins.
