01

First, picture the problem.

A model trained only on plausible RTL examples does not directly learn which implementation best meets a particular physical-design objective.

↳Train a delivery planner with different preferences: save fuel, arrive sooner, or use a smaller vehicle.
02

Make it concrete.

Interactive · toy example

Candidate A has toy power 2 and delay 6. Candidate B has power 5 and delay 2. Select the preference used to rank the pair.

Power objective prefers A because 2 < 5. Delay objective prefers B because 2 < 6. The preferred/rejected pair changes with the objective; these numbers do not reproduce the paper’s reward formula.

Invented teaching example. Values and costs here are not measurements from the paper.

04

Bring the idea to ABCD.

A possible research direction
PPA objective→Tool-derived preference pairs→Adapted RTL generator

Distinguish changing ABCD’s prompting or search from changing model weights using physical-design feedback.

Small experiment: For identical functional tasks, compare an area-focused generator with a delay-focused one. Report both correctness and cross-objective costs.

Keep in mind: Training toward an objective does not enforce a hard budget on every output. The evaluation flow and technology remain part of the result.

This connection is an interpretation for the project, not an implementation or performance claim by the paper.

05

Read the result in context.

Trade-off settings
7 objectives

The study considers individual PPA goals and combinations of them.

Average area improvement
29.05%

Reported for SFT-plus-preference training versus SFT-only in the study; it is not a universal improvement guarantee.

Scope matters. The reported power, performance, and area averages summarize different objective settings. They should not be interpreted as simultaneous gains on every design.
