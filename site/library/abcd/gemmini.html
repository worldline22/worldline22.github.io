01

First, picture the problem.

Evaluating a compute block in isolation misses shared-memory contention, address translation, operating-system effects, and software overhead.

↳A faster train does not guarantee a shorter trip if the station transfer becomes slower.
02

Make it concrete.

The faster block can lose end-to-end

Interactive · toy example

Toy system A takes 8 ticks in compute and 2 elsewhere. System B takes 4 in compute but 8 in transfers and software. Choose the measurement scope.

Compute-only: B wins, 4 < 8 ticks. End-to-end: A totals 8 + 2 = 10; B totals 4 + 8 = 12. This invented example shows why a kernel speedup need not become a system speedup.

Invented teaching example. Values and costs here are not measurements from the paper.

03

What did the paper add?

01

Parameterize hardware

Generate variants of the compute array, memories, and related accelerator structures.

02

Provide a software stack

Expose programming interfaces for running DNN workloads.

03

Integrate and evaluate

Use full SoCs to study shared resources and cross-stack behavior.

The contribution: An accelerator generator and integrated hardware/software environment for systematic DNN architecture evaluation.

04

Bring the idea to ABCD.

A possible research direction
Generated accelerator→Runnable software + SoC→System-level measurements

A successful ABCD output should include runnable workloads and integration artifacts when making system-level claims.

Small experiment: Compare accelerator-only and application-level timing before and after a memory-system change. Explain any reversal in the winning design.

Keep in mind: Keep fabricated-chip evidence, FPGA-accelerated simulation, and individual case-study results labeled separately.

This connection is an interpretation for the project, not an implementation or performance claim by the paper.

05

Read the result in context.

End-to-end evaluation
5 DNNs

The paper evaluates popular DNN workloads using the FireSim simulation platform.

Fabricated implementations
16 / 22 nm

Gemmini-generated accelerators were also realized in TSMC 16 nm and Intel 22 nm processes.

Scope matters. Fabrication demonstrates realizability; it does not mean every reported benchmark is a direct measurement on those chips. The full-stack evaluations have their own methodology.
